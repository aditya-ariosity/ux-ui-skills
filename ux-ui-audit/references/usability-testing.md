# Lightweight Usability Testing

Use this when expert inspection cannot establish user behavior or when the team needs validation before or after a change. The goal is learning, not statistical proof.

## Frame the study

Write one sentence for each:

- **Decision:** what product decision will this evidence change?
- **Target users:** who genuinely performs the task, including relevant access needs?
- **Critical task:** what realistic outcome will participants attempt?
- **Risk:** what behavior or misunderstanding is most costly if wrong?
- **Evidence:** what observed behavior would support or challenge the hypothesis?

Prefer a small, focused round over a broad script. Five participants is a useful starting point for many qualitative studies, not a universal law. Recruit enough variation to cover materially different roles, devices, expertise, language, and access needs. Run additional rounds when risks or user groups differ.

## Write non-leading tasks

Give participants a believable situation and goal. Do not name the control, feature, or expected route.

```text
Weak: Click Export and download a CSV.
Better: You need to analyze last month's orders in another tool. Show me how you would get the data.
```

Avoid teaching during the task. Ask neutral follow-ups such as `What are you looking for?`, `What did you expect?`, and `What would you do next?`

## Run the session

1. Confirm consent, recording, privacy, and right to stop.
2. Explain that the product is being tested, not the participant.
3. Establish relevant context and prior experience without turning the interview into a survey.
4. Observe the task. Record actions, words, pauses, errors, recovery, and completion.
5. Ask for interpretation after consequential moments; do not rescue too early.
6. Debrief on confidence, unmet needs, and the participant's expected alternative.

Include disabled participants and assistive technologies in normal research rather than deferring accessibility to a final compliance pass.

## Analyze behavior

Create an observation table:

`Participant | Task moment | Behavior or quote | Consequence | Hypothesized cause | Confidence`

Prioritize repeated or severe breakdowns, but do not count mentions as survey percentages. A rare event can still be critical when harm is high. Separate observed behavior from interpretation, and connect findings to a product decision.

## Convert learning into action

Report:

- the three most consequential observed patterns;
- supporting and contradictory observations;
- which design assumptions changed;
- the smallest product change worth testing next;
- unresolved questions and recruitment gaps;
- the next test round and success signal.

Do not produce a long issue inventory. Fix the most serious problems, then test again.
