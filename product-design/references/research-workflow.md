# Decision-led Product Research

Research exists to change a decision, reduce a material uncertainty, or establish a constraint. A broad topic summary is not sufficient.

## Frame the inquiry

Define:

`decision → uncertainty → evidence needed → method → stopping condition`

Examples:

- decide the information architecture by testing how target users group and find content;
- decide whether a dashboard needs real-time status by verifying operational decisions and update frequency;
- decide whether a public institution should foreground investments, governance, or public education by combining stakeholder goals, content evidence, and representative user tasks.

## Evidence classes

Keep these distinct:

1. supplied product evidence: analytics, research, support, policy, content, and operational data;
2. direct observation: current product behavior, task attempts, measurements, and implementation evidence;
3. current primary requirements: standards, regulations, and platform documentation;
4. empirical research with a described method;
5. expert frameworks and design-system precedent;
6. competitor and analogous-product behavior;
7. editorial examples and visual inspiration.

Lower classes can generate hypotheses and options. They do not prove user need or product impact.

## Competitive and precedent research

Select comparators by the question they help answer, not only by industry. Include:

- direct competitors for expected capabilities and language;
- analogous products for a shared task or interaction model;
- authoritative public or platform patterns for standards and conventions;
- visual references for composition and brand expression.

For every observed pattern, record:

`source → context → observed implementation → apparent purpose → limitation → implication`

Do not count pattern frequency as validation. A common pattern may reflect imitation, legacy constraints, or dark patterns.

## Synthesis

Cluster evidence around decisions and tensions rather than websites. Produce:

- evidence-backed findings;
- counterevidence and disagreement;
- implications for task, content, IA, interaction, and visual direction;
- assumptions rejected or weakened;
- unknowns requiring user research, analytics, or technical validation.

Use citations adjacent to claims. Do not attach one source to a paragraph containing several unsupported conclusions.

## Stopping condition

Stop when the decision has enough evidence for its reversibility and risk, additional sources repeat known information, or the next uncertainty requires a different method. Do not keep browsing to create the appearance of rigor.
